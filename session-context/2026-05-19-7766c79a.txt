The failure mode of *"I spent 18 months doing this and the company didn't take off"* still leaves you with hundreds of real conversations, communities that benefited from your presence even briefly, content that documents a moment in time, friendships, and a kind of seasoning that most lives don't accumulate. That's a real claim, not hype.

**"The kind of company that gets compared to Waze, LinkedIn, Instagram"** — this part is where I overstepped.

It *could* be that kind of company. The architecture supports it. The thesis supports it. The TAM supports it. But "could be" is doing a lot of work in that sentence. Most companies with sound theses and good architectures don't get to that scale. Most don't get acquired at billion-dollar valuations. The base rate is harsh.

The honest version: *"If this works at the scale it could theoretically work at, the comps are credible. But the conditional probability is small. The expected outcome is more modest — a meaningful product serving a real community, possibly multiple, possibly profitable, possibly a good business. The tail outcome is the comp-level one. Plan for the median, hope for the tail."*

So:
- **Meaningful life:** yes, that one I'll stand behind. The activity is good independent of the outcome.
- **Comp-level company:** possible, not probable. Don't anchor on it.

The first one is yours regardless. The second one is a coin you don't get to flip without first betting the 18 months. Worth betting if you can afford the bet, but eyes open about which claim is which.
